The "Cheers" system is being developed by "Wine Ha'Emek," a wine trading company, to manage key business operations, including inventory management, customer management, and order processing and delivery.

Orders can be categorized as either regular or urgent and include the following details: order number, order date, current status (in process, dispatched, delivered, paid, suspended, or canceled), shipment date, and the assigned employee's information (ID, name, phone number, office address, email, and employment start date). The actual delivery of orders is managed by an external company, called "GalGalim," and falls outside the scope of the current system.

Orders may consist of multiple wines in different quantities. To minimize delivery costs, customers can place joint orders. In such cases, the system must track which wines and quantities have been ordered by each customer. For each customer in an order, the system records their ID, name, phone number, delivery address, email, and the date of first contact with the company. Company employees are allowed to order wines under the same conditions as regular customers. 

Urgent orders are processed with priority and must be placed by a single customer to ensure prompt delivery. When placing an urgent order, the priority level (a number between 1 and 5) and the expected delivery time must be specified. Regular orders, on the other hand, allow multiple customers to combine their wine selections into a single order to minimize delivery costs. In such cases, the system tracks the specific wines and quantities ordered by each customer, as well as the main customer who is responsible for the entire order.

For every customer included in an urgent or regular order, the system records their ID, name, phone number, delivery address, email, and the date of first contact with the company. 

Each type of wine is identified by its name and serial number. Examples of wine types include red, white, sparkling, fortified, rosé, and dessert wines. Additional details for each wine type include food pairings; In addition to the name of the dish, up to 5 links (URLs) to possible recipes will be kept. Marketing employees can also recommend occasions suitable for drinking each wine type, providing a general description of the occasion (e.g., marriage proposal, nature hike, cheese night), along with the season and location (indoor/outdoor).

Each wine is produced by a single manufacturer. For each manufacturer, the system will store the full name, contact phone number, address, and email. A unique automatic identifier is assigned to each new manufacturer. For each wine, the system will capture the name, description, production year, price per bottle, sweetness level (dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet, sweet), and product image. Wines are distinguished by their manufacturer number and catalog number. Marketing staff will link wines to their types and can modify price details and product images. Each wine is stored in a specific location (e.g., main refrigerator, cellar), and each storage location has a unique number and name. The system tracks the number of bottles of each wine at each storage location (wines may be stored in multiple locations and a single location may store multiple wines).
